How a duel works

Rounds. Each round you and your rival act at the same moment. Your rival has already chosen by the time you write, and neither of you sees the other's choice until the round plays out. Take as long as you like to think.

Write and cast. Describe your spell and press Cast. Claude reads it and judges its power. Power 1 to 3 lands this round, 4 to 6 the next, 7 to 9 the round after, and 10 takes four rounds. While you channel you can't cast anything else, and a hard hit can stagger or break the spell. Within a round, weaker spells land first.

Wild magic. Claude rates how inspired each spell is, from ✦ to ✦✦✦✦✦. A flat "fireball" is weak. A vivid, surprising spell that plays off the battlefield or your rival can be two and a half times as strong. Inspired spells sometimes surge: they overcharge, echo across the battlefield or give mana back. Bland ones can lash back at you. Recasting the same idea fades its inspiration.

Cataclysms. Describe magic that would plainly end a mage and it becomes a cataclysm: slow, costly and fatal if it lands. Any breaking spell shatters it while it gathers, so ward yourself first or strike while your rival is busy. Your rival will feel it coming.

Lasting magic. Spells can leave something behind: burning and poison that hurt every round (and ignore wards), silence that stops your rival casting anything new, frost that slows their casting, curses that weaken their magic. On yourself: mending, haste and swelling power. Effects show under each mage's life bar with the rounds they have left. Your rivals use them too.

Gather focus. Skip your turn and recover extra mana.

The battlefield remembers. A flood, a ring of fire, storm clouds, grasping roots: spells that create conditions leave them on the floor for a few seconds. A spell that uses one becomes a combination and hits half again as hard. Use two or more and it is a grand combination: it tears through wards and shatters whatever your rival is channelling. Your rival can use your conditions too. Some pairs react: lightning through a flood becomes Chain Lightning, a gale through fire becomes a Firestorm, shadow over light an Eclipse.

Reading your rival. Once your rival has been channelling for a round you see the colour of the magic they gather. A long channel means something big. Break it, ward against it, or burn away the conditions it needs.

Your grimoire. Every spell you write is kept. Recast it with one tap; each recast in the same duel costs a little more mana, so new ideas stay stronger than old ones.

Quick sigils. Spark, Shield and Snap cost nothing to think up and are deliberately weak. Use them while you learn, or when you are stuck.

Usage. Each new spell you write asks Claude once. Recasts, sigils and your rivals cost nothing.
